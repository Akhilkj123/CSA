### Types of SOC Model
- **Internal SOC Model** - An in-house/internal SOC model is recommended for those organizations that have security issues related to outsourcing. Also, to those organizations that have a budget to invest as internal SOC requires 24/7 efforts.
- **Outsourced SOC Model** - In this model, a Managed Security Service Provider (MSSP) sets up the infrastructure and offers threat intelligence and other capabilities. Its initial start-up cost is low because it spends mainly on the vendor's infrastructure. Here, service providers have multiple connections with different clients through which they can develop a sound knowledge base and repeat
the process of detecting and escalating security threats and vulnerabilities.
- **Hybrid SOC Model** - It is a combination of both in-house and outsourced SOC models. In this model, the organization is accompanied by MSSP to offer the most secure approach. Its initial start-up cost is the same as the internal SOC, but it also includes the cost of vendor staffing during office hours.

### Components of SOC Maturity Models: 
- **People** - This component emphasizes the skills, expertise, and staffing of the SOC team.
- **Continuous Improvement** - Continuous improvement in a SOC context involves regularly refining and enhancing processes, tools, and skills to adapt to evolving threats.
- **Threat Intelligence Integration** -  Utilizing diverse, reliable sources of intelligence and ensuring it is relevant to the organization’s context.
- **Collaboration and Communication** - Ensuring smooth information flow between analysts, managers, and other SOC team members.
- **Process** -  Well-defined procedures for detecting, analyzing, responding to, and recovering from security incidents.
